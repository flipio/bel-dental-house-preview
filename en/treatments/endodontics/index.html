Endodontics

Root canal treatment aimed at preserving the tooth and its root. The procedure is painless, under local anaesthesia.

About the treatment

When is root canal treatment needed?

Endodontics is the fundamental conservative procedure for treating root canals, with the aim of preserving the tooth and its root.

The procedure begins with removing the affected tooth tissue and the inflamed pulp, followed by chemical and mechanical cleaning of the canal. Because of the specific and complex anatomy of the canals inside the root, the canal length has to be determined precisely.

To measure the length and to monitor the cleaning and filling of the canal we use a dedicated electronic device — an apex locator — and small digital X-rays that show the structure of the root at minimal radiation.

I.

The aim

To preserve the tooth and its root by removing the inflamed pulp and the infection from the canal system.

II.

Precision

Canal length is measured with an electronic apex locator, and cleaning and filling are monitored with small digital X-rays at minimal radiation.

III.

Afterwards

A well-treated tooth can serve the patient for many years and can later carry a crown or a bridge.

The process

  1. 01

    Diagnostics

    Clinical examination and a digital X-ray of the tooth.

  2. 02

    Access and cleaning

    Removal of the affected tissue and inflamed pulp, followed by chemical and mechanical cleaning of the canal.

  3. 03

    Measurement and filling

    The canal length is determined with an apex locator and the canal system is filled.

  4. 04

    Restoring the crown

    The tooth is closed with a filling or restored with a crown, depending on how much tooth structure remains.

Frequently asked questions

Does root canal treatment hurt?
The procedure is performed under local anaesthesia and is painless.
How long does a treated tooth last?
A well-treated tooth can serve the patient for many years and can later be used to carry a bridge or a crown.
