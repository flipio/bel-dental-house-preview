About the treatment

When is bone regeneration needed?

Bone regeneration is a surgical method whose aim is to increase bone structure and volume, so that an implant can be placed afterwards.

It is used wherever bone is lacking, whether one tooth, several or all of them are missing.

Guided bone regeneration is based on specialised bone substitute materials and methods, in which the material is gradually infiltrated and replaced by the patient’s own bone. Depending on the material and the method, the process takes 4 to 6 months, after which treatment continues with implant placement.

I.

Socket preservation

Regeneration after an extraction: a bone substitute is placed into the socket left by the root, reducing the biological loss of bone.

II.

Ridge augmentation

Widening a narrow bony ridge so that an implant can be placed, while preserving the neighbouring natural teeth.

III.

Sinus lift

Where there is substantial bone loss in the posterior upper jaw, a substitute material is placed through a small window in the sinus wall, raising the sinus floor.

The process

  1. 01

    Assessment

    Scans and an assessment of the quantity and quality of bone at the planned implant site.

  2. 02

    Procedure

    The bone substitute is placed using the chosen method, under local anaesthesia.

  3. 03

    Healing

    The material is gradually replaced by the patient’s own bone; the process takes 4 to 6 months.

  4. 04

    Implant placement

    Once regeneration is complete, treatment continues with implant placement.

Frequently asked questions

How long does bone regeneration take?
Depending on the material and method, the process takes 4 to 6 months, after which treatment continues with implant placement.
Is regeneration always necessary?
No. For an edentulous jaw the All-on-4 concept is often possible, which avoids bone grafting for most patients.
